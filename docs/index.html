Where financial statements are attached, the deadline is the earlier of two dates: the annual return date plus 56 days, or the financial year end plus nine months plus 56 days.

01 / The rule people miss

Two rules runat the same time.Only one binds.

Most directors know the 56-day rule. Fewer know there is a second calculation running alongside it, and that for many companies it produces the earlier date — the one that actually binds.

Rule one Annual return date + 56 days. The one everyone has in the diary.
Rule two Financial year end + 9 months + 56 days. Applies wherever financial statements are attached.
Which applies Whichever falls earlier. For a company whose year end sits well in front of its annual return date, rule two bites first.

The second rule is not an edge case. It applies to every company that attaches financial statements to its annual return — which is almost all of them.

And beyond the penalty

01

Late filing penalty

€100 the day after the deadline, then €3 per day, capped at €1,200 per return.

02

Loss of audit exemption

Lost where a company files late more than once in a five-year period.

03

Prosecution of the company and its directors

Failure to file is an offence under section 871 of the Companies Act 2014, with a Class A fine of up to €5,000 on summary conviction. The CRO may also impose an on-the-spot fine for persistent late filing.

04

Involuntary strike-off

The Registrar may begin strike-off proceedings. Once dissolved, company assets vest in the Minister for Public Expenditure and directors may be personally liable for debts incurred while the company stands dissolved.

Under section 22 of the Companies (Corporate Governance, Enforcement and Regulatory Provisions) Act 2024, a company loses the audit exemption if it files late more than once in a five-year period. This replaced the earlier position, under which a single late filing cost the exemption for the following two years.

04 / Starting one

Four company types.Pick the one thatfits the structure.

The type you incorporate determines how many directors you need, whether the secretary can also be a director, and what the members are exposed to. It is worth getting right at the start.

LTD

Private Company Limited by Shares

The most common company type for small and medium enterprises.

Directors
Minimum of one director
Secretary
The company secretary cannot also be the sole director
Liability
The shareholders have limited liability

Secretary

Has no share capital. Typically used by not-for-profits, representative bodies, sporting and charitable organisations, and owner management companies.

Company law moves. A note written while something was still a Bill is worth re-reading once it is an Act.

Correction

A single late filing no longer costs you the audit exemption

Under section 22 of the Companies (Corporate Governance, Enforcement and Regulatory Provisions) Act 2024, audit exemption is lost where a company files late more than once in a five-year period — not on a single late filing.
