Cessation of a company

Voluntary strike-off for a company that has ceased to trade or never traded — or an introduction to a liquidator where that is the better route.

Applies toCompanies that have ceased trading or never traded
Triggered byOnce the company has stopped trading and its affairs are settled
DeadlineThe Revenue letter of no objection must be dated within three months of the application. The newspaper notice must be placed within the 30 days before it.
NoteObtaining the Revenue letter has been taking roughly 12 weeks, so an annual return date falling inside that window needs planning for.
If missedA company left non-compliant rather than closed properly may be struck off involuntarily, with the consequences that carries for its directors.
AuthorityCompanies Act 2014

Where a company has ceased to trade, or has never traded at all, it can apply to the Registrar to be voluntarily struck off the register. Four conditions must be satisfied: all outstanding annual returns delivered to the CRO; assets and liabilities of not more than €150; a letter of no objection from the Revenue Commissioners dated no more than three months before the application; and a notice of intention advertised in one daily national newspaper no more than 30 days before it.

The sequencing is the difficult part. The Revenue letter has been taking around twelve weeks to obtain, and it expires three months after it is issued, so the whole application has to be planned backwards from it — and a company whose annual return date falls inside that window still has to file.

Alternatively the company can be liquidated, and we can put you in contact with a liquidator. We prepare the application to the Companies Registration Office to strike the company off.
