Annual return and compliance filings

The annual return and financial statements, filed on time, every year, with the accountant kept in the loop.

If missed€100 the day after the deadline, then €3 a day to a maximum of €1,200. Audit exemption is lost where a company files late more than once in a five-year period. Failure to file is an offence and can lead to involuntary strike-off.
AuthorityCompanies Act 2014 · CRO

Every Irish registered company must file an annual return with the Companies Registration Office each year, regardless of whether it has traded or lain dormant. The return sets out the directors, the secretary and the shareholdings as at the date of the return, and in most cases the financial statements go with it.

The deadline is the part people get wrong. It is not simply the annual return date. Where financial statements are attached, the filing deadline is the earlier of two dates: the annual return date plus 56 days, or the financial year end plus nine months plus 56 days. For companies whose year end sits well before their annual return date, the second rule bites first — and it is routinely missed.

We file the annual return, liaise with your accountant so that the financial statements are ready in time, and prepare the annual general meeting documentation required to approve and receive the accounts.
