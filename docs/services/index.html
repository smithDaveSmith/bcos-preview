The register

What a company owes, and when.

Sixteen entries. Three of them have a statutory clock attached, and those are the ones that carry a penalty. Each entry says who it applies to, what sets it running, and what happens if it is missed.

Formation

Bringing a company into existence and giving it the two things the State requires it to have: an address and a secretary.

01 Incorporation of a new company Choosing the right company type, preparing the constitution and the statutory documentation, and completing the registration with the Companies Registration Office. AppliesAnyone setting up a business in the Republic of Ireland → 02 Registered office services A registered office in Dublin for your company, with post forwarded in whatever manner you specify. AppliesEvery Irish registered company → 03 Provision of company secretary Beacon acts as the named company secretary, attends board meetings, prepares the minutes and advises on company law and corporate governance. AppliesEvery Irish company. For a single-director company, the secretary must be a different individual or a corporate body. →
4 entries

Standing obligations

The work that recurs. These are the entries with a clock attached, and the ones that carry a penalty when they are missed.

04 Annual return and compliance filings The annual return and financial statements, filed on time, every year, with the accountant kept in the loop. ClockWithin 56 days of the annual return date — or the financial year end plus nine months plus 56 days, whichever falls earlier. If missed€100 the day after the deadline, then €3 a day to a maximum of €1,200. Audit exemption is lost where a company files late more than once in a five-year period. Failure to file is an offence and can lead to involuntary strike-off. → 05 Creation and maintenance of company registers The statutory register, set up properly and kept current as directors, secretaries, addresses, shareholdings and beneficial owners change. AppliesEvery Irish registered company → 06 Register of beneficial ownership Filing and maintaining the company’s beneficial ownership details on the central register, and keeping the company’s own internal RBO register. ClockFive months from incorporation for a new company. Fourteen days for any change of beneficial owner. If missedFailure to comply can result in the Registrar commencing prosecution proceedings against the company. → 07 Company secretarial audit and review A full review of the company’s regulatory and procedural position against current legislation and best practice. AppliesCompanies that have grown, changed hands, changed advisers, or lost track →
8 entries

Changes to the record

A company changes constantly — its people, its name, its shares, its constitution. Each change has to be minuted, recorded and notified.

08 Appointment, resignation and removal of directors and secretary Minuting the change, notifying the Registrar and updating the statutory register — the three steps that have to happen together. AppliesEvery Irish registered company → 09 Registration of a business name Registering the name a company or an individual actually trades under, where it differs from the registered name. AppliesA company or individual trading under a name other than its registered name → 10 Change of company name Members' approval, a new constitution carrying the new name, and a change of name certificate from the Registrar. AppliesAny company changing its registered name → 11 Alterations to the company constitution Drafting and adopting a revised constitution, and filing it with the Companies Registration Office. AppliesAny company whose share structure, share classes or governance arrangements are changing → 12 Changes in share capital Allotments, transfers, new share classes and the filings and register updates that follow them. AppliesAny company issuing, transferring or restructuring shares → 13 Golden share Creating a share that controls the composition of the board — commonly used to form a group structure for intercompany lending. AppliesGroups putting a holding structure in place → 14 Redemption and buyback of shares A company acquiring its own shares — subject to distributable reserves and to the constitution permitting it. AppliesCompanies buying back or redeeming shares from an existing shareholder → 15 Re-registration of a company Converting from one company type to another — no longer requiring High Court approval. AppliesCompanies changing type, for example from an LTD to an unlimited company or a DAC →
1 entry

Ending

Closing a company down cleanly, so that it does not end up struck off with liabilities outstanding.

16 Cessation of a company Voluntary strike-off for a company that has ceased to trade or never traded — or an introduction to a liquidator where that is the better route. ClockThe Revenue letter of no objection must be dated within three months of the application. The newspaper notice must be placed within the 30 days before it. If missedA company left non-compliant rather than closed properly may be struck off involuntarily, with the consequences that carries for its directors. →
